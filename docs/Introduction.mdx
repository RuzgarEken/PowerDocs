# What is Power

Power is a **content-agnostic stat and effect system** for Unity. You assemble stat-driven mechanics by connecting assets in the Inspector, with little or no custom code.

Power is **User Friendly**
- Create game mechanics by connecting Power assets.
- Most mechanics can be built without writing custom code.

Power is **Stat Agnostic**
- There are no predefined stats in Power.
- A Stat's purpose and behavior are fully defined by the user through its relationships.

Power is **Effect Centric**
- Power does not just support primitive stats like `float` and `int`.
- It provides a variety of useful stat types to enable complex effects.

Power **Minds Its Own Business**
- Power does not force you to use its systems across your entire game.
- It operates within a well-defined scope, allowing you to extend it freely in your own game logic.

Power **Supports Both Turn-based and Real-time Systems**
- This is mainly about pacing, and Power supports both approaches.
- You can even combine both if you want.

### How it fits together

Power's runtime flow has five elements: **Actor**, **Stat**, **Effector**, **Effect**, and **Processor**.

<PowerFlow />

An Effect targets a **Target Stat** and can carry **Attribute Stats** that define its behavior or classification. As the Effect passes through the Processor funnel, relationships defined on those Stats — such as modifiers, immunities, limits, and reflections — can shape, modify, or block the result before it is applied.

## Is Power for you?

Power is for you if you want to design your game with ready-made, detailed building blocks instead of having to implement complex stat and effect mechanics from scratch.

#### Games Power is good for:

- Action RPGs
- JRPGs
- Tactics / SRPGs
- Roguelikes & roguelites
- Looter shooters
- Tower defense
- Survival & crafting
- City & management sims
- Idle & incremental games

Every game has stats and ways for players to change them. That interaction is exactly what Power focuses on, making it a strong fit for genres built around numbers, stats, and the rules connecting them
